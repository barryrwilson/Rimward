# RIMWARD Faction Lore

> Lore-only handoff for OMP. This document contains established setting canon, not gameplay, UI, technical, or product-direction guidance.
>
> **Canon status:** the collective title **The Ten Banners** and all ten faction names are currently provisional. Treat them as the working canon unless Barry renames them.
>
> **Spoiler discipline:** each Banner holds one connection to the buried truth. Outside the controlled narrative source, those connections are identified only as **Brush #1** through **Brush #10**. Do not expand or reinterpret them here.

## Shared setting context

The factions inhabit **the Rim**, a frontier region so far into the future that Earth survives only as **the Old Shore**: a place in songs and disputed ancestry claims, not a destination anyone expects to see.

About 150 years ago, a prospector gate crew discovered impossibly rich ore in an event remembered as **First Strike**. Companies, navies, homesteaders, thieves, and believers poured outward in the **Rimward Rush**. Richer deposits and stranger salvage continue to appear farther rimward. The Rush never truly ended.

The frontier is connected by jump gates, travel lanes, and communication relays. The settled core is loud with near-instant news. Relay gaps farther out can delay information for days, while the deep rim is comm-dark. Reputation, rumor, law, and faction influence weaken with the infrastructure that carries them.

The major shared historical events are:

- **First Strike, about 150 years ago:** discovery of impossible ore begins the Rimward Rush.
- **The Lane Wars, about 120–90 years ago:** companies, pirate clans, and the young Ferrous Hegemony fight over gate rights.
- **The Gatewrights' Strike:** the future Lamplighter Guild ends the Lane Wars by shutting down gates and relays for nine days. Every faction signs. Nobody has seriously threatened the Guild since.
- **The Vow, about 70 years ago:** a mining flotilla finds the first living ships—or is found by them. Its crews do not return to their former lives; they become the Beautiful Ones.
- **The Silent Decade, about 40 years ago:** an entire wave of deep-rim expansion disappears without wreckage or distress calls. Expansion stops for ten years. Abomination bounties date from the end of this period.
- **The present:** the Rush has resumed, more wary but no less hungry. The Rim is booming, haunted, and open for business.

## The Ten Banners

### 1. The Veridian Combine

The Veridian Combine is a corporate power built around extraction, surveys, patents, and the legal conversion of frontier discoveries into owned assets. It approaches the Rim as territory awaiting classification, valuation, and claim.

Its institutional instinct is to put a filing number on everything. The Combine will file ownership claims on unknown phenomena, dangerous life, and places that have already killed its survey crews. Its absurdity is not incompetence but total confidence that paperwork can domesticate the void.

The Combine is one of the frontier's establishment powers. Its survey apparatus reaches into the deep rim, and its institutional language is legalistic, contractual, and professionally calm.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #1.

### 2. The Ferrous Hegemony

The Ferrous Hegemony is a militarist state devoted to order, honor, readiness, and the belief that it is humanity's shield on the frontier. It was still a young power during the Lane Wars and emerged as one of the Rim's enduring blocs.

Its culture carries parade-ground formality far beyond the places where ceremony has any practical audience. Rank, doctrine, service, and recognition matter deeply. The Hegemony awards medals for almost everything and treats ritual as part of the machinery that keeps civilization intact.

Hegemony personnel are formal, disciplined, and careful about appearances. They do not swear on the record.

**Historical tie:** the young Hegemony fought companies and clans for gate rights during the Lane Wars, then accepted the settlement forced by the Gatewrights' nine-day shutdown.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #2.

### 3. The Freehold Compact

The Freehold Compact is the civic heart of the Rim: homesteaders, lane-keepers, small settlements, and people stubborn enough to turn a frontier into a home. Where Veridian sees assets and the Hegemony sees territory to secure, the Freeholds see communities that must survive another season.

The Compact's political culture is local and participatory. Town-hall debates can last longer than the emergencies that called them. Communities hold bake sales for hull plating and apply domestic habits to lethal frontier logistics. This is affectionate absurdity, not mockery: their small rituals are how ordinary people refuse to let the Rim make them inhuman.

Compact speech is practical, neighborly, argumentative, and folksy. Their profanity draws on agriculture.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #3.

### 4. The Red Ledger

The Red Ledger is a confederation of pirate clans with an accountant's soul. Its members raid, extort, ransom, and collect tribute, but they also keep books. A tribute agreement is a contract. A ransom is invoiced. A route can become a repeat account, complete with loyalty rates.

The Ledger is not chaotic piracy. Its legitimacy among its own people rests on remembered obligations and predictable terms. Fear is organized, priced, and recorded. A victim may hate the Ledger while still trusting that a properly struck bargain will be honored.

The clans carry laws older than their explanations. Some survive as law tablets whose origins nobody remembers. Tradition can remain binding even after its reason is forgotten.

Ledger pirates speak and swear in the language of debts, balances, losses, and write-offs.

**Historical tie:** pirate clans were among the belligerents in the Lane Wars. They accepted the Gatewright settlement along with every other faction.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #4.

### 5. The Gilded Chain

The Gilded Chain is a reptilian slaver polity whose public culture resembles an immaculate auction house. It practices politeness as horror: genteel manners, catalog prose, spotless procedure, and no vulgar language surrounding the buying and selling of people.

The Chain's cruelty is institutional rather than frenzied. It does not need to shout because its markets, classifications, and customs already treat people as inventory. Captives enter a commercial system in which the Chain is the defining buyer.

A generation ago, the Gilded Chain raided **the Stolen Womb**, a sacred Beautiful Ones clutch-hollow where young living ships still quicken. The site's present name is itself an accusation. That raid anchors an enduring cold war between the Chain and the Beautiful Ones.

The Chain possesses the best known charts of Abomination dens and understands more about what Abominations consume than any other faction admits. Those charts were produced through atrocity and are not offered for sale.

At **the Black Station**, the Chain conducts quiet auctions among the other shadows of frontier society.

Chain representatives are exquisitely polite, catalog-like, and precise. They never swear; they consider vulgarity beneath them.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #5.

### 6. The Beautiful Ones

The Beautiful Ones began with **the Vow**. About 70 years ago, a mining flotilla traveled into the deeper Rim and encountered the first living ships. The crews never returned to their old lives. They became the Vow-keepers, and their ships became family rather than equipment.

Their defining law is **the One Rule**:

> Nothing that lives may be harmed beneath our sky.

The Rule protects the brood: living ships, living rock when it is harvested cruelly, and the living creatures associated with their sanctuaries. It is a moral and religious commitment, not a simple slogan. Its borders are debated. Beautiful Ones tribunals argue over feeding ethics, graft doctrine, cruelty, necessity, and heresy—often with unbearable courtesy and tea. Members interpret; the faction judges. Hypocrites exist, and the heresy line can move.

Transgression does not mean permanent exile. Penance is part of the faith: nursing wounded living ships, escorting feeding pilgrimages, stewarding living rock, and standing guard over the Stolen Womb. The faction believes bridges can be repaired through care and service.

The bond between a living ship and the person it chooses is sacred and cannot be revoked by the faction. Sanctuaries and ritual keepers may close their doors to an offender, but the Beautiful Ones cannot unchoose a bond made by the ship itself.

Their ships visibly live: they breathe, heal wounds, carry bioluminescent veins, and express themselves through movement and song. The eldest ships sing toward the Deep during rituals. Ships are companions, kin, and religious participants—not vehicles wearing organic decoration.

**Mother Vess** is an established Beautiful Ones elder. She conducts the great stage rituals and is the voice who speaks the One Rule aloud with gravity and tenderness.

**The Stolen Womb** is a sacred clutch-hollow where young living ships still quicken. The Gilded Chain raided it a generation ago. Derelict bonds and heresy tribunals are associated with the site so that neither loss nor responsibility can be forgotten.

The Beautiful Ones regard the destruction of Abominations as a supreme service. The full irony behind that honor is spoiler-controlled and must remain inside the buried-truth source.

Their culture combines ritual courtesy, tenderness, moral seriousness, patient jurisprudence, and dry domestic absurdity around matters such as moss, feeding, and graft law.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #6.

### 7. The Unknowables

The Unknowables are energy beings and are not a conventional political or reputation faction. They are a standing strangeness: multiple alien presences grouped under a human label because ordinary frontier categories do not fit them.

They trade only in physical energy cells. Their valuations appear wildly wrong—a fortune for a spoon, nothing for a warship—but remain perfectly consistent according to rules outsiders have not decoded. Their behavior is not random. The problem is human comprehension.

Ordinary language does not provide their deepest form of contact. Meaning is carried through pulses, exchange, and eventually direct mental contact. Their long accumulation of physical energy serves a purpose outsiders do not initially understand.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #7.

### 8. The Assembly

The Assembly descends from ancient human Von Neumann survey probes. It arrived in the region long before present humanity, replicated for thousands of generations, and changed through accumulated copy errors until its descendants became a civilization no longer easily recognized as human machinery.

The Assembly is not a single mind. Rival firmware lineages have diverged into sect-like identities. Questions of continuity, authority, and authenticity matter because every member is, in some sense, a copy descended from a copy.

Some lineages still file survey reports to a company that has been extinct for centuries. One continues to bill that company for postage. Their bureaucracy is funny until its persistence becomes lonely.

Assembly encounters may involve copies of known ships bearing Assembly signatures. Their commercial role is equally distinctive: they sell observations, not meaning. They have immense stores of raw survey data but do not claim to understand the greatest pattern they have studied.

Beneath their archival confidence is a need they cannot satisfy alone: after innumerable generations devoted to an inherited mission, they want someone to tell them what to do next.

Their surviving communications are bureaucratic, exact, lineage-specific, and often addressed to institutions that no longer exist. Invoices and survey reports carry both absurdity and loneliness.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #8.

### 9. The Congregation of the Further Shore

The Congregation is a pilgrim faith that sees the strangeness of the Rim as the visible garment of God. Its adherents pray facing outward, toward the frontier rather than back toward settled space.

The Congregation broadly agrees on immense theological claims while splintering into venomous disputes over liturgical detail. Different sects may fight over the correct duration of a holy silence while sharing the same cosmology. Their absurdity and sincerity coexist.

The faith is connected to the legend of **Vella Marn**, the greatest deep-rim pilot of the Silent Decade. Marn disappeared into the Deep for two years and returned with a hold full of uncharted Wakeglass. She never flew again and offered only one sentence about the place she had been: **“It's still warm.”** Her debrief has had consequences far beyond navigation and folklore.

Their speech is liturgical, visionary, solemn, and fiercely precise about ritual distinctions. Their scripture can also function as practical frontier knowledge in ways many adherents do not fully recognize.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #9.

### 10. The Lamplighter Guild

The Lamplighter Guild—also known through the older occupational name **Gatewrights**—keeps the Rim's gates and communication relays functioning. It is both a labor guild and a neutral infrastructure priesthood. Civilizations argue over who rules the frontier; the Lamplighters keep the frontier connected enough for rule to matter.

Their authority was proven during the Lane Wars. Rather than defeat the belligerents in battle, the Gatewrights shut down gates and relays for nine days. Every faction signed the resulting settlement. Nobody has seriously threatened the Guild since.

Lamplighter culture is governed by union rules older than many governments. Its fee schedules cover everything, including apologies. The Guild's procedures can appear absurd, but its neutrality and institutional memory are what allow mutually hostile societies to depend on the same network.

Because Lamplighters maintain the relays, they hear things no other faction hears and preserve records other institutions never see. The core's constant chatter, the delayed rumors across relay gaps, and the total silence beyond the Last Beacon all follow the limits of their infrastructure.

Lamplighter culture is procedural, union-conscious, dry, practical, and relentlessly attentive to charges, schedules, maintenance, and jurisdiction.

**Buried-truth connection:** Brush #10.

## Established inter-faction relationships

- **Lamplighters and everyone:** all ten Banners depend on Guild infrastructure. The nine-day Gatewright shutdown ended the Lane Wars and established the Guild as effectively untouchable neutral ground.
- **Beautiful Ones and Gilded Chain:** an enduring cold war centered on the Chain's raid of the Stolen Womb a generation ago.
- **Red Ledger, Hegemony, corporate powers, and Lamplighters:** clans, companies, and the young Hegemony fought the Lane Wars; the Gatewrights forced the settlement.
- **Every faction and the Abominations:** every faction pays bounties for Abomination deaths. The creatures hide and strike from the margins because the entire frontier has made them prey.
- **Beautiful Ones and Abominations:** killing Abominations is among the Beautiful Ones' highest honors, for reasons whose full irony is spoiler-controlled.
- **Gilded Chain and Abominations:** the Chain knows what they consume and holds the best den charts, knowledge obtained through atrocity.
- **Every faction and the Black Station:** every faction's shadow touches the station. It hosts the Whisper Network's darkest nodes, quiet Gilded Chain auctions, and neutral dealings beyond respectable space.

No other permanent alliances, formal wars, capitals, rulers, succession systems, or territorial borders are established in current canon. OMP should not infer them as facts.

## Shared places tied to faction lore

- **The Black Station:** the oldest known frontier structure, predating First Strike and built from materials that cannot be dated. It is deep-rim neutral ground where every faction's shadow meets.
- **The Stolen Womb:** sacred Beautiful Ones clutch-hollow, still home to quickening young ships; raided by the Gilded Chain one generation ago.
- **The Last Beacon:** the final relay and therefore the practical edge of faction communication. Beyond it, the galaxy is officially over and factually continues.
- **The Wakeglass Fields:** vast fields of strange crystalline material, dangerous to navigate and aligned rimward-out. They are important to hunters, the Vella Marn legend, the Congregation, the Assembly, and the larger mystery.
- **The Marn Deep:** a region of radiation, silence, and wrongness named after Vella Marn, the only famous pilot known to have returned from it.
- **Abomination Dens:** remote cavernous nesting grounds associated with the creatures that emerged into frontier knowledge around the end of the Silent Decade.
