- **Volume breakdown:** Saucer disc (~40%); elongated secondary/engineering hull (~35%); two nacelle cylinders (~20%); narrow connecting neck (~5%).
- **Detail hierarchy:** Large forms are clean and read clearly from distance; medium detail is the engraved panel lines and small sensor arrays on the saucer rim; fine greeble concentrates at the nacelle bussard domes at the forward ends. The broad dorsal saucer surface is mostly smooth.

- **Transferable move:** Narrow the connecting neck between two large primary volumes to roughly 30% of each volume's widest point — the pinched waist separates habitation from engineering at a glance and keeps the two masses reading as independent zones. Tagged: `frigate`, `heavy`.

## Block observations

- **Extreme fore:aft mass contrast is the clearest directional read.** The Sulaco (66) and Venator (73) both place almost all their volume at the fore and then allow the aft to dissolve — the viewer immediately knows which way the ship travels without any other cue.
- **A single horizontal accent stripe at the hull equator unifies any smooth disc or oval hull.** The Prometheus (67) demonstrates this; one stripe at the widest point reads at combat distance and separates upper crew zone from lower drive zone.
- **Window banding at two deck heights around a perimeter simultaneously communicates habitation and scale.** Voyager (64) and the Sovereign class (72) use this reliably; it works at any ship size and is cheap geometry.
- **Uniform surface tiling — whether armor ribs, overlapping scales, or hemispherical pits — reads as faction language.** The Battlestar Galactica (68) uses transverse ribs, the Geth Dreadnought (78) uses overlapping elliptical scales, and the MC80 (74) uses random pit tiling; each is a single repeated primitive that covers the hull completely and distinguishes the design without any color markings.
- **A cluster of large spherical engine pods at the stern creates an unambiguous propulsion signature.** The UNSC Strident (77) uses three blue-plasma spheres that read at maximum render distance; circular cross-sections at the stern contrast strongly against any elongated hull form.
- **Deeply recessed cockpits on disc hulls read as armored rather than exposed.** Slave I (75) demonstrates that sinking the cockpit below the hull equator and surrounding it with the disc rim communicates protection without any explicit armor geometry.
- **An asymmetrically-placed cockpit or engine pod creates an instantly memorable silhouette.** The Raptor's (69) starboard engine pod and the Jedi starfighter's (76) corner cockpit both break the centreline symmetry in a purposeful way that no other ship in this block repeats — each reads uniquely from every angle.
- **Organic tension-line ridges can replace all panel lines and greeble to communicate speed and non-human origin.** The Vulcan ship (71) uses only smooth raised flow lines running nose-to-aft; this is transferable directly to Beautiful Ones or Unknowables faction language where hard mechanical panels are prohibited.
