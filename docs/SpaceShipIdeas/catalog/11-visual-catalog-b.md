## Block observations

- **Expanse vs. Halo mass distribution:** Both Donnager and the Pillar of Autumn use rectangular slab mass distributed along the full hull length, but The Expanse applies copper-banded zone breaks while Halo applies uniform dark panel density without tonal variation. The banding approach is more immediately readable at RIMWARD game distances.
- **Smooth versus dense as faction language:** Covenant (index 23) and Imperial Cutter (index 29) use near-zero greeble on very large hulls. Both instantly read as high-status or non-human. All human military ships in this block (UNSC, Donnager, Viper) use extreme surface density. Smooth hull = faction identity marker in this block.
- **Racing cigar geometry:** The Razorback (index 18) demonstrates that a very long cylinder with a ventral sled plate is readable as a personal high-speed craft from a single side view. The key is extreme length-to-diameter ratio — at least 8 : 1 — combined with a single lateral engine glow at the stern.
- **Logo-sourced geometry:** Two images (indices 30, 31) are faction logos rather than ship renders. Both logos contain geometry directly usable for ship bows: the Amarr twin-horn arc for a split prow, and the Caldari nested ring for a toroidal engine mount. Faction logos frequently encode ship design language in simplified silhouette form.
- **Blue interior light as luxury or non-human signal:** The Imperial Cutter (index 29) uses blue light visible through hull apertures to communicate a premium interior. The Covenant Carrier (index 23) has no visible interior light and reads as sealed and alien. Blue interior light through openings reliably signals habitation quality without adding surface detail.
- **Stripe as hierarchy anchor:** Three ships in this block (Razorback red stripe, Type-9 orange stripe, Colonial One navy stripe) use a single large bold stripe to carry visual weight and faction identity on an otherwise plain hull. In each case the stripe is wider than a simple racing pinstripe and carries the brand or paint-blocking function.
- **Scale via contrast object:** Colonial One (index 20) uses a window row running the full fuselage length as the primary scale signal. The Anaconda (index 26) uses the surrounding hangar bay walls. The Donnager (index 17) uses structural ring spacing. For RIMWARD ships without a player ship nearby, a repeated human-scale element (window row, door outline, railing) is the reliable fallback scale cue.
