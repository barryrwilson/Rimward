## Block observations

- Three of the sixteen images are 2D faction logos (indices 32, 33, 35) and one is an interior set photograph (index 47); nearly 25 % of this batch contains no ship exterior. Always verify image content before building a design reference — a logo URL can appear adjacent to ship URLs in a source list.

- The organic ships in this batch (Vorlon transport, Shadow vessel, Wraith hive ship) share no structural vocabulary with mechanical ships, but they cluster around one principle: surface texture is the entire design. Remove texture from the Shadow vessel or Wraith hive and nothing remains. This is the inverse of mechanical design, where greeble adds to a legible primary form.

- All four Stargate and both Babylon 5 mechanical entries use aft-heavy mass distribution — the largest structural mass sits at the stern, and a lighter body or spine extends forward. This makes the travel direction immediately legible from any viewing angle, without labels or arrows.

- Luxury versus utility divides cleanly along surface treatment in this batch. The 890 Jump uses very large panels, smooth areas, and a single continuous accent stripe to communicate wealth. The Drake Caterpillar uses many small panels, visible structural straps, and heavy weathering to communicate economy and function. Both strategies are fully explicit and could be applied directly to RIMWARD faction briefs — contrast Gilded Chain with Independent or Freehold Compact.

- The Hiigaran Battlecruiser demonstrates the three-slab stack technique: layer three horizontal hull plates and step them outward at the stern. Each slab contributes its own independent silhouette line, so the ship reads as complex from both the side and the top without requiring any surface greeble.

- Warm amber-orange is the genre default engine-glow colour across this entire batch — Serenity's twin outboard pods, the Caterpillar's engine block, and the Hiigaran engine bells all use it. The Ha'tak uses orange-amber as its primary emissive signal on the pyramid faces. The one departure is the Carrack's blue nose blister and Destiny's blue spine stripe. Using teal, violet, or cold white as an engine glow will immediately read as alien, exceptional, or Unknowable in the RIMWARD context.

- The Destiny's extreme spine-and-body ratio — a near-skeletal forward needle roughly one third of total ship length — shows how far a spine can extend before legibility breaks. The technique holds because the spine and the dense aft mass are visually distinct in both geometry density and surface value; the two zones contrast enough to read as a single integrated ship rather than two unrelated objects.
