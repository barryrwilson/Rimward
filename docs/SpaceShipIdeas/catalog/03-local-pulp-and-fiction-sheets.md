## What this set teaches

- **Sphere-on-spine is a complete grammar for a long-voyage ship.** Discovery One (F-024–026, 030–031) proves it: crew sphere at fore (~20% of length) + slender truss spine (~75%) + small engine cluster at stern (~5%). This proportion set transfers directly to any long-haul freighter or heavy-class build where travel direction must be instant.

- **The finned torpedo is the oldest legible rocket.** From the 1926 Amazing Stories covers (C-035), Soviet stamps (C-051–052), the Linemar toy (F-038), and the Space Patrol comic (F-011), every era independently converges on the same form: cylinder body, swept base fins, pointed nosecone. It reads as "rocket" at thumbnail size with zero labelling.

- **Flying disc is the universal non-human shorthand.** The Soviet stamp saucers (C-031, C-036, F-007–008) are flat discs with a single central dome. The Buck Rogers disc (F-022) adds rim-mounted nozzles. Together they establish that a flat disc with no visible drive = implied alien or field propulsion. Use it for the Unknowables; never for Ferrous or Congregation.

- **A large sensor dish at one end instantly designates the craft as a probe or communicator.** Cassini (C-001), Voyager (C-006), Dawn (C-002–004, C-018) all share this: dish is the largest single element; the body is secondary. If the dish faces fore, it is listening; if it faces away from travel direction, it is broadcasting.

- **Panel colour alone changes faction read without changing structure.** The same ISS truss structure with dark blue panels (C-055) versus purple panels (C-057) reads as two different organisations. Colour is faster than shape at faction assignment distance.

- **Repeating equal spine bays give instant scale.** The Discovery One truss (F-025) has roughly 15 equal bays. A viewer counts the bays and infers length without being told. Use equal bay repetition on any freighter or frigate spine to communicate size purely through geometry.

- **A split aft section signals two drive systems or captured components.** The EVE Gallente frigate (F-021) bifurcates at the stern into two prongs at ~60°. This reads as "two engines, perhaps from two different ships." Use it for Red Ledger to show assembled capture hardware.

- **An oversized forward aperture on a cylinder is the minimal science-instrument read.** Hubble (C-059–060, C-063–064) achieves its identity from one feature: the large circular opening at the fore end. Veridian and Assembly scouts can borrow this — a faceted or circular fore-aperture immediately communicates sensing, not shooting.

- **The V-nose or cleft prow concentrates threat forward.** The Colonial Viper (F-049), BC-303 blueprint (F-018), and coil-beam craft (C-040) all push the most distinctive geometry to the nose. Whatever is at the nose — sensor, weapon, or boarding spike — defines what the ship does. Build the nose first.

- **Rim-distributed propulsion (Buck Rogers disc, F-022) implies omnidirectional movement.** Radial nozzles around a disc edge mean the craft does not have a preferred travel direction. Reserve this for ships that manoeuvre unpredictably — the Unknowables cutter or an Independent omnidirectional scout.

- **A smooth closed thermal-shell surface (SIRTF, C-065–072; Gilded Chain brief) is legible as protective concealment.** A drum with no visible seams and flush surfaces reads as "something hidden inside." The SIRTF encapsulation sequence shows this in eight consecutive shots. The Gilded Chain ceramic-armour brief is the direct fictional application.

- **Pulp colour palettes are distinct from photographic and digital-game palettes.** Amazing Stories uses flat saturated poster colours (red, turquoise, gold, black) with no gradients. Soviet stamps use the same flat graphic language. These palettes do not exist in EVE Online or AstroMenace screenshots. If a ship is lit with pulp-palette colours — flat, primary, high-contrast — it reads as retro, populist, and optimistic rather than industrial or threatening.

## Pulp shape vocabulary

Counted across all pulp magazine covers, Soviet space art, toys, and retro-fiction images in this set (C-028 to C-052, F-007–008, F-011, F-012, F-022, F-035, F-038).

| Hull primitive | Description | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Finned torpedo | Smooth cylinder + swept or straight base fins (3–4) + pointed nosecone; vertical launch form | **9** (C-028 model, C-035 rocket, C-051/052 each ~4 rockets in stamp, F-011 Space Patrol comic ~2, F-038 Linemar toy) |
| Flat saucer with central dome | Thin disc, height <15% of diameter, dome at centre-top; no visible drive | **8** (C-031 fleet ~6 individual discs, C-036 ×1, F-007 ×1, F-008 ×1) |

| Disc with rim nozzles | Flat disc with rocket nozzles distributed around the outer rim; propulsion is radial | **1** (F-022 Buck Rogers) |
| Elongated beam-weapon fuselage | Long cylinder with a large cylindrical coil/capacitor section aft of mid-body; beam projector at nose | **1** (C-040 coil-beam) |
| Sphere-on-antenna (satellite) | Spherical pressure vessel with 4–8 thin whip antennae; Sputnik archetype | **3** (C-051/052 stamps ×2 each, F-007/008 background) |
| Multi-legged lander drum | Squat cylinder with structural outrigger legs; no wings or fins | **1** (F-012 Amazing Stories 1963) |

**Summary:** The dominant vocabulary is the **finned torpedo** (9) and the **flat saucer** (8). Every other form appears at most once or twice. Any retro-fiction or Soviet-space-inspired design that does not use one of these two forms will look atypical for the era and should do so deliberately.
