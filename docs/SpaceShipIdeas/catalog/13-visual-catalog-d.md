## Block observations

- **Organic ships exploit talon geometry.** Moya (56) uses three downward-curving stern claws to simultaneously mark the drive location, suggest biological life, and create an unmistakable rear silhouette at distance. A single set of three convergent fins achieves more read value than a dozen conventional tail surfaces.
- **Non-ship images dominate the block.** Four of sixteen entries are logos, badges, magazine pages, or title cards (48, 52, 57, 62) rather than ship renders. These still contain transferable iconographic moves — the badge silhouette at index 62 is particularly compact as a function communicator.
- **Offset drive nacelles create visual interest.** The Musai (51) hangs a single large engine pod off a swept arm from the port bow, breaking bilateral symmetry and making the ship read from multiple angles without redundancy. This move works for any faction whose builds suggest improvised or captured assembly.
- **The ring thruster is a universal read.** Both the Aurora (61) and the White Base (50) place large circular ring drives as mid-ship elements visible from any angle. A centred ring immediately tells the viewer where the drive is without requiring a side view.
- **Monolithic darkness communicates freighter scale better than greeble does.** The Dune Heighliner (59) is nearly featureless, yet reads as larger than any greeble-heavy ship in the block because its dark slab mass occludes the planetary sky. Scale by volume blocking, not by surface complexity.
- **Concentric groove patterns earn disc-hull depth cheaply.** The USS Discovery (60) applies engraved concentric rings to its saucer dorsal face; this gives the large smooth surface a sense of precision engineering and internal decks with minimal mesh cost.
- **Procedural catalog grids reveal the minimum geometry set.** The No Man's Sky developer screenshot (58) shows ~63 fighters that share one core mid-wing body type.
