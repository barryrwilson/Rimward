# Visual catalog A — indices 0–15

Star Wars (starwars wiki) and Star Trek (memory-alpha wiki) reference block: disc freighters, triangular wedge capitals, single-seat fighters, saucer-nacelle exploration ships, and two alien outliers.

### 0 — Millennium Falcon (starwars)
- **Image:** https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/starwars/images/5/52/Millennium_Falcon_Fathead_TROS.png/revision/latest?cb=20221029015218
- **Source page:** https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Millennium_Falcon
- **Silhouette:** A flat disc/saucer with a forward twin-mandible fork and a lateral port-side cockpit arm; disc family.
- **Proportions:** approximately 3:2 length-to-width ratio with very shallow height (roughly 6:1 length-to-height); mass concentrates at the mid-disc, with the mandible fork pulling attention fore.
- **Volume breakdown:** main disc hull ~65%; twin forward mandibles ~15%; aft engine block (three circular vents) ~10%; lateral port cockpit arm and pod ~10%.
- **Detail hierarchy:** the dorsal and lateral surfaces are uniformly busy at three scales — large hull-panel sub-forms (roughly 30+), medium-size hatches and stacks, and fine conduit detail; a circular radar dish sits atop center-aft; two large oval access hatches on the dorsal surface are the clearest scale anchors; a dorsal quad-gun turret is center-aft; very few smooth areas — only the three engine bell interiors are clean.
- **Surface + colour:** off-white / light gray primary hull; scattered red-oxide accent panels appear in irregular blotchy clusters (replacement panels, not stripes); panel lines are dense and irregular; surface is uniformly weathered and matte with soot gradients around mechanical openings.
- **Light language:** three circular blue-glow engine vents at the aft; a small red running light on the port cockpit arm; no visible window bands — the ship does not advertise habitation; emissive area share is very small (~3%).
- **Function reads:** the twin mandibles at the bow read as cargo loading or docking equipment; the large disc diameter relative to height implies a cargo hold beneath the dorsal skin; the dorsal gun turret signals self-defense; the off-center cockpit arm codes a modified civilian hull rather than a purpose-built military frame.
- **Scale cues:** oval access hatches sized for one adult; the cockpit viewscreen is just wide enough for two crew side by side; the radar dish is roughly one-eighth the disc diameter; landing gear recesses are visible on the ventral face.
- **Transferable move:** Build a disc freighter from a broad, flat-bottomed cylinder; cut a forward U-slot to create mandibles; offset the cockpit pod to port on a short horizontal arm so the command position never sits on the centreline — this asymmetry codes civilian origin and individual modification. Tag: `freighter`, `independent`.

### 1 — Imperial I-class Star Destroyer (starwars)
- **Image:** https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/starwars/images/9/9d/ImperialStarDestroyer-RFGE.png/revision/latest?cb=20240803160040
- **Source page:** https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Imperial_I-class_Star_Destroyer
- **Silhouette:** A perfect isoceles-triangle wedge seen from slightly above and aft-port, with a stepped dorsal superstructure rising to a command tower at mid-ship; wedge family.
- **Proportions:** approximately 4:1 length-to-max-width from above; the stepped superstructure adds roughly 25% of total hull height at its peak; mass is distributed evenly across the wedge slab, with the tower as the highest mid-ship landmark.
- **Volume breakdown:** main wedge hull slab ~70%; stepped dorsal superstructure terraces ~20%; command tower block with two spherical sensor globes ~7%; aft engine cluster (not visible in this view) ~3%.
- **Detail hierarchy:** the entire surface is greebled at three consistent scales — large hull plates, mid-size equipment and weapons blocks, and fine conduit/vent texture; the dorsal surface is busier than the lateral faces; the tower with its two paired spherical globes is the single vertical landmark; warm amber interior lights punctuate the terraced superstructure densely; no smooth areas are visible anywhere.
- **Surface + colour:** medium-dark gray hull, slightly lighter on the dorsal ridge; no colour markings or recognition bands at this scale — identity comes from shape alone; the command tower globes are symmetric and clearly paired.
- **Light language:** dozens of amber/warm-white interior window lights distributed across the terraced superstructure; no prominent running lights visible in this view angle; emissive area is small and scattered but reads as inhabited.
- **Function reads:** the pointed bow is an aggression signal; the tiered superstructure hosts sensors and weapons batteries; the two spherical globes at the tower read as powerful scanning arrays; the flat ventral surface implies a hangar bay; the overall form signals command authority and approach control over traffic.
- **Scale cues:** window pin-points on the superstructure terraces are the only scale cues; at render distance they imply enormous hull size; no docked craft visible.
- **Transferable move:** Build a wedge from a flat right-triangular prism; step the dorsal face into three or four terraces rising from bow to mid-ship tower — each terrace hosts one class of equipment and makes the authority hierarchy readable from above at any distance. Tag: `frigate`, `ferrous`.

### 2 — X-wing starfighter (starwars)
- **Image:** https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/starwars/images/0/00/Xwing-ROOCE.png/revision/latest?cb=20230516042654
- **Source page:** https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/X-wing_starfighter
- **Silhouette:** A narrow central fuselage with four S-foil wings deployed in an X-formation, each wing carrying one engine nacelle at the tip; cruciform family.
- **Proportions:** fuselage roughly 5:1 length-to-width; wing-tip-to-wing-tip span slightly exceeds fuselage length; height across the X is roughly equal to span; mass concentrates at the engine section (aft mid-fuselage) where all four nacelle roots converge.
- **Volume breakdown:** main fuselage torpedo ~40%; four engine nacelles at wingtips ~30%; four flat wing panel slabs ~20%; nose sensor and torpedo cone ~10%.
- **Detail hierarchy:** 2–3 large forms (fuselage, wing-set, engines); medium forms include the canopy fairing, the droid socket behind the canopy, and each engine barrel; small forms include blast deflectors and surface conduits on the wing panels; the cockpit and droid-socket zone is the most visually dense area; the wing panels are relatively clean flat surfaces carrying only a subtle dark grid texture.
- **Surface + colour:** off-white / cream fuselage; red/maroon diagonal stripe accents on the wing panels; dark blue/gray solar-collector grid on wing surfaces; heavily weathered — scorch marks and dirt accumulation at panel edges and engine exhausts.
- **Light language:** no window glow from this above angle; four engine nacelles would fire blue plasma exhaust (none active in this static pose); four laser cannon barrels at wingtips are the primary threat signal; droid-socket status lights are tiny; emissive area in static pose approaches zero.
- **Function reads:** four guns at four wingtips = fighter; single small canopy = one pilot; R2 droid socket behind canopy = autonomous navigation capability; no visible cargo volume; engine mass at wingtips signals high roll inertia balanced by quad drive thrust.
- **Scale cues:** the canopy is just large enough for one seated pilot; laser cannon tubes at the wingtips are thumb-diameter relative to the wing chord; the droid socket is just large enough for a cylindrical barrel-shaped unit.
- **Transferable move:** Place one engine nacelle at each wingtip of a cruciform layout — the engines are the visual punctuation that reads at distance while the wing panels read at mid-range, creating a two-level recognition hierarchy that works from any approach angle. Tag: `ace`, `light`.

### 3 — TIE/ln space superiority starfighter (starwars)
- **Image:** https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/starwars/images/d/d1/TIE_Fighter_DICE.png/revision/latest?cb=20161021230639
- **Source page:** https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/TIE/ln_space_superiority_starfighter
- **Silhouette:** A spherical cockpit pod suspended at the exact mid-span of two rigid pylons connecting two large hexagonal flat wing panels; dumbbell family (horizontal, with sphere at centre).
- **Proportions:** panel-to-panel span is approximately 2.5:1 relative to the sphere diameter; the sphere sits precisely centred; the entire assembly is very flat — essentially two-dimensional from the front; mass is at the geometric centre.
- **Volume breakdown:** central cockpit ball ~25%; two large hexagonal wing panels (each roughly 30% of overall volume projected area) ~55%; two pylon/strut assemblies ~15%; small attachment hardware at pylon-sphere junction ~5%.
- **Detail hierarchy:** the cockpit ball is moderately detailed — six small hemispherical bumps around its equator, one forward-facing hexagonal viewport opening; the wing panels are almost entirely smooth flat planes with a subtle fine-grid texture (solar cell array); the pylons are the busiest zone with mechanical brackets, structural ribs, and connection hardware where they meet the ball; overall greeble is very low compared to any Star Wars ship with a hull.
- **Surface + colour:** dark charcoal gray on the ball and pylons; the wing panels are flat black; no colour markings or recognition bands; the entire ship is monochromatic gray-black.
- **Light language:** one hexagonal forward viewport on the cockpit ball, dark in this static image; no engine exhaust visible from the front angle; no running lights; emissive area is essentially zero — the ship reads as dark and unlit.
- **Function reads:** the two large flat wing panels are the primary propulsion signal (ion drive / solar collection); the sphere is clearly the entire crew volume (room for one pilot); no cargo capacity, no docking collar, no visible weapons from this angle; the absence of any secondary systems reads as a disposable military asset.
- **Scale cues:** the hexagonal viewport on the ball is sized for one face; the sphere appears just large enough for a single seated pilot; no other scale cues exist on the surface.
- **Transferable move:** Suspend a small spherical crew pod on two horizontal rigid struts between two large flat panel wings — the contrast between the organic sphere and the rigidly geometric flat panels creates immediate visual tension; the ball becomes the only place a viewer reads as inhabited. Tag: `ace`, `light`.

### 4 — YT-2400 light freighter (starwars)

- **Proportions:** disc roughly 1.3:1 length-to-width; very flat height (height approximately one-eighth of width); the command arm adds roughly 25% extra length to the port-fore quadrant; mass is at the mid-disc.
- **Volume breakdown:** main oval disc hull ~60%; port-fore command arm and cockpit pod ~15%; aft cylindrical engine/nacelle cluster ~15%; slight ventral bulge (lower forward hull) ~10%.
- **Detail hierarchy:** the disc surface carries large segmental panel arcs and medium-sized equipment housings: two circular vent-dome pairs at the fore-top, a raised dorsal gun turret ring at centre, a recessed rectangular cargo/crew hatch at centre-aft; the command arm and cockpit sphere are the most detailed sub-zone with small red antenna points at the tip; large red stripe accents divide the hull into zones rather than adding fine greeble; the surface is less busy than the Falcon.
- **Surface + colour:** aged tan / beige primary hull; red horizontal stripe accents in two bands (mid-hull rim and an outer zone); panel-arc seams define large disc segments; matte and heavily weathered.
- **Light language:** a small circular viewport at the cockpit arm tip; a small red running light at the very arm extremity; the gun turret has no active glow; emissive area is minimal.

- **Scale cues:** the cockpit viewport is just wide enough for one or two crew; the cargo hatch is roughly one-eighth of the disc diameter; the turret ring is sized for a single gunner.
- **Transferable move:** Build a complete oval disc hull with no forward slot or mandibles, then attach the cockpit as a separate spherical or cylindrical pod at the end of a short arm off the port-fore quadrant — the disc reads as pure cargo volume and the arm clearly separates navigation from freight. Tag: `freighter`, `independent`.

### 5 — EF76 Nebulon-B escort frigate (starwars)
- **Image:** https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/starwars/images/7/77/EF76NebulonBEscortFrigate-CGSWG.png/revision/latest?cb=20241213054232
- **Source page:** https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nebulon-B_escort_frigate
- **Silhouette:** A large lenticular forward pod connected by a very long thin spar to a smaller aft engineering block; below the spar hang rows of docked smaller craft; dumbbell-and-spine family.
- **Proportions:** overall length roughly 5:1 relative to the forward pod's max width; the forward pod is roughly one-third of total length and two-thirds of total height; the spar is very thin relative to either pod; mass sits forward; the aft block is roughly one-quarter the forward pod's volume.
- **Volume breakdown:** forward lenticular crew/medical pod ~40%; multiple launched or docked fighter-class craft visible hanging below the spar ~20%; aft engineering block with angular fin mounts ~20%; central connecting spar ~15%; vertical spike antennae above the forward pod ~5%.
- **Detail hierarchy:** the forward pod has large smooth curved hull sections punctuated by clusters of blue window lights and several vertical spike antennae projecting upward; the spar is smooth with a row of blue viewports along one face; the aft block is angular with equipment clusters and a small command structure; the docked fighter-class craft below the spar are individually readable and serve as the primary scale reference — each is roughly the size of a large fighter, and there are more than a dozen visible.
- **Surface + colour:** medium gray / silver hull on both pods; relatively clean and lightly detailed by Star Wars standards; no colour accent bands; blue point-light windows are the primary surface feature.
- **Light language:** clusters of blue window lights on the forward pod and aft block; individual blue lights along the spar; the docked craft below each carry their own small blue lights; no engine glow in this static view; emissive area share is moderate (~8%), concentrated in window clusters.
- **Function reads:** the large forward pod with rows of docked craft hanging below reads as a carrier-hangar and crew habitat; the very long thin spar deliberately separates the crew area from the engines (medical ship logic — drive radiation kept far from patients); the aft block is clearly propulsion and power; the spike antennae signal command-level sensor capability.
- **Scale cues:** the docked fighter-craft below the spar are the clearest scale anchor — each is fighter-class; the blue window clusters on the forward pod are individual crew-deck windows; the entire assembly is demonstrably much larger than the craft attached to it.
- **Transferable move:** Connect two radically different-sized hull pods with a spine/spar far thinner than structural logic would normally require — the tension between the thin connection and the heavy end masses makes the design read instantly as intentional functional separation, not poor engineering. Tag: `frigate`.

- **Transferable move:** Edge-light a large vessel with evenly-spaced running lights along every hull boundary — the dots read the complete silhouette at distance without requiring any surface detail; they also imply that the hull edges are far apart, communicating scale before any other cue. ## Block observations

- **Three dominant silhouette families:** Star Wars splits into disc freighters (Falcon, YT-2400) and triangular wedge capitals (Star Destroyer, Executor); Star Trek Starfleet ships use a third family throughout — the saucer-on-neck-with-remote-nacelles dumbbell; these three strategies cover 14 of 16 entries, with the Borg cube and TIE Fighter as the two structural outliers.
- **Weathering is a faction signal, not a scale signal:** every Star Wars ship in this block is heavily weathered with irregular replaced-panel accents; every Starfleet ship is clean to lightly worn regardless of size; the Klingon and Romulan ships read as dark rather than dirty; this means finish quality communicates faction allegiance before shape communicates class.
- **Emissive strategy splits the two universes:** Star Trek ships concentrate glow in a small number of large, bright, colour-coded apertures (deflector dish, nacelle tips, warp aperture) that function as landmarks at distance; Star Wars ships carry almost no sustained emissive area at rest — the TIE Fighter has no running lights at all; the Borg cube is the sole Trek design that uses distributed surface glow as the primary visual language.
- **Smooth zones make large ships readable:** Star Trek hulls leave large smooth saucer and hull-slab zones between functional clusters; these smooth zones separate the operational zones visually and make the ship read clearly at distance even with dense window detail; Star Wars hulls pack greeble at every scale uniformly, which means they read more richly up close but flatten into texture at thumbnail distance.
- **Deliberate illegibility is itself a design move:** every Star Wars and most Star Trek ships communicate at least one function instantly from silhouette alone (fighter = cruciform wings + four engines; carrier = thin spar with visible docked craft; shuttle = tall upright fin); the Borg cube is the only entry that systematically removes all functional legibility, all directional cues, and all scale anchors — and that absence is the design statement.
- **Asymmetry codes individual ownership:** both disc freighters (Falcon, YT-2400) use an off-centre cockpit arm as the primary signal of individual modification rather than institutional construction; every wedge, saucer, or military design in this block is bilaterally symmetric; asymmetry in hull geometry is the fastest cue for civilian or independent origin.
- **Scale-cue discipline predicts reading distance:** Star Wars ships rely on access hatches and cockpit viewports sized for one human body, which requires close viewing to resolve; Star Trek ships rely on hull lettering and visible window-row bands that read at greater distance; the Executor and Borg cube both deliberately suppress scale cues — for different reasons but with the same effect of making the viewer feel disoriented about size.
