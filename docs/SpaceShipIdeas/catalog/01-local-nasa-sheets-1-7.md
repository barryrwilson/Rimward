## What this set teaches

- **Dish-dominant probe grammar works as a design system.** Dawn, Cassini, Voyager, Planck, and Kepler all place a large sensor aperture as the primary silhouette mass. At game distance this reads instantly as an instrument vessel. Use this for Assembly daughter probes and Veridian survey heads: make the aperture 40–60% of the silhouette.

- **The spine principle is real and scalable.** The 1969 artificial-gravity spacecraft (N-031) and the Saturn V family both show that a long axis with minimal girth change reads as a transit or cargo vessel. A spine is not a freighter by itself, but adding discrete cargo/habitat rings along it creates a freighter without adding bulk.

- **Stepped volumes communicate crew location.** In the Apollo CSM and the SLS capsule stack, the crew volume is the smallest, topmost element. This gives the viewer a clear answer about scale and priority. Use this rule for any ship that needs a command point: make it the narrowest, highest-positioned zone.

- **Internal circular sections generate strong interior greeble.** The Skylab MDA cross-section (N-021, N-022) shows that packing a 3 m diameter circle with labeled racks and boxes reads as believable and purposeful from any angle. The trick is radial symmetry broken by a few asymmetric items (tape recorder vs viewfinder position). Apply this to Assembly and Ferrous interiors.

- **Repeating panel modules establish faction language.** Dawn's solar panels (N-005, N-009, N-060) are always the same physical cell tiling at two scales: the cell tile and the panel tile. This creates a visual system that looks mass-produced without being monotonous. This is the clearest real-world example of the Assembly "self-similar module" rule.

- **Tool marks, straps, and brackets read as lived-in.** Skylab interiors (N-025, N-026, N-027) show orange nylon straps, adhesive labels, and cable harnesses. These are not decorative; they result from zero-g use. Freehold and Lamplighter ships need exactly this kind of purposeful mess: straps, clamp brackets, and cable routing along accessible paths.

- **A solar sail teaches extreme surface-to-mass ratio.** The solar sail concept (N-017, N-018) has a 1000:1 area-to-payload ratio. This is a useful boundary marker: it shows how far the Congregation sail or Unknowables energy plane can be pushed before the payload disappears. The structural grid seams on the sail panels also show how to texture a large flat surface without banding.

- **The Space Shuttle is the best argument for black-on-white thermal language.** Tile coverage on Columbia (N-045, N-046) divides a white ship into white-top and black-bottom cleanly. This is directly applicable to any faction ship that needs clear upper/lower differentiation without paint markings: thermal logic alone produces the separation.

- **Cleanroom photography shows real-scale hardware joins.** N-053 (Dawn cylinder flanges), N-047 (GPM array frame), N-049 (PUNCH satellites) all show actual bolt-and-flange hardware at human scale. Model these for the Assembly faction at 0.3–0.5 m scale on an in-game cutter hull and the detail will read correctly at camera distances a few ship-lengths away.

- **Three nozzles in a triangle cluster read as propulsion at a glance.** The SSME cluster close-up (N-043) shows that three nozzles in a tight triangular arrangement is instantly understood as a propulsion rear. The ribbed bell texture is the secondary cue. Use this for any faction that uses conventional chemical or ion propulsion at the engine section; the triangle cluster convention is universal enough to read without text.

- **Ion engine glow is a wide blue fan, not a rocket spike.** N-063 shows the Dawn ion plume is wide, translucent, and gradually brightens from the nozzle outward. It is nothing like a rocket exhaust. For Assembly and Veridian ships using ion propulsion, the correct emissive shape is a wide conical haze, not a tight flame jet.

- **Scale contrast needs a human figure or a known object nearby.** Every cleanroom photograph in this set (N-024, N-052, N-053, N-055) becomes readable only because technicians stand beside the hardware. In-game, the player ship beside a freighter provides the same function. Never remove the player from a docking approach scene.
