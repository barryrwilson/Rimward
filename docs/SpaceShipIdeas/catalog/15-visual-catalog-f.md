### 80 — Fer-de-Lance (elite-dangerous)

- **Image:** https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/elite-dangerous/images/4/4d/Fer-de-Lance-ship-top-view.png/revision/latest?cb=20181128113824
- **Source page:** https://elite-dangerous.fandom.com/wiki/Fer-de-Lance
- **Silhouette:** An extremely flat, wide delta that tapers aggressively from mid-aft to a fine needle prow — wedge family.
- **Proportions:** Approx 4:2.5:0.5 (L:W:H); mass concentrates at the mid-to-aft third, where the hull is widest and thickest before the wings thin to their tips.
- **Volume breakdown:** Main flat delta body (~50 %); swept lateral wing panels (~25 %); needle nose spike (~10 %); raised dorsal cockpit/module at mid (~10 %); aft engine cluster (~5 %).
- **Detail hierarchy:** Two or three large forms; roughly five medium panel groups; greeble concentrated at the dorsal cockpit and turret housing. The wings and nose are nearly smooth. The center-top surface carries all the busy detail.
- **Surface + colour:** Bold two-tone: saturated red fills the dorsal spine and cockpit surround; cream-white fills the wing/flank panels. Panel lines are clean chamfered edges with no visible weathering. A single red stripe traces nose to engine along the centerline.
- **Light language:** Small rectangular cockpit glazing centered on the dorsal ridge. One single-barrel weapon turret sits mid-dorsal on a low mount. Red recognition stripe doubles as a paint axis. Engine glow not visible in this view.
- **Function reads:** The extreme flat cross-section reads as low radar profile or high-speed atmospheric penetrator. Wide swept flanks suggest high-agility moment arm. The single dorsal turret signals a light combat/dueling role rather than a broadside platform.
- **Scale cues:** The cockpit glazing panel (small, single-pilot scale) is the only direct size reference. This reads as a one-person combat craft.
- **Transferable move:** Position the widest point of the wing sweep at 60–70 % aft from the nose tip; this creates a "coiled mid-lunge" tension in the silhouette without requiring extra length. Tag: `ace`, Red Ledger.

## Block observations

- **Radial petal geometry is the strongest non-humanoid silhouette available.** The Thargoid Interceptor (index 81) uses eight identical arms rotated around a central boss; at thumbnail size it is instantly distinguishable from every manufactured design in this block. Six to eight arms in radial symmetry with a compact hub should be the first-choice move for any faction that must read as non-human at distance.

- **Physically separating propulsion from crew into distinct geometric volumes is the most reliable scale grammar.** The Constitution class (index 94), the Freelancer (index 85), and the Sojourner (index 96) all place drive and hab in completely separate shapes. When propulsion and crew read as different objects, the viewer immediately deduces both function and relative scale without labels or text.

- **Twin parallel forward elements — prongs, pods, or nacelles — create an aggressive bow posture cheaply.** The Rifter (index 82), the Argama (index 90), and the Cutlass Black (index 84) all use paired forward geometry. Two prongs that bracket the nose add no significant polygon cost and read as "grasping" or "boarding-ready" head-on at any distance.

- **A dense regular window grid is the most efficient capital-ship scale signal.** The Stellaris mammalian fleet ships (index 86) use identical small square windows in strict horizontal rows as their only surface language; the grid alone communicates habitation decks, crew count, and era without greeble or colour variation. This pattern transfers directly to Ferrous Hegemony frigates and freighters.

- **A monochromatic warm tile-panel hull reads as ancient empire without any heraldry.** The EVE Apocalypse (index 83) covers its cigar hull entirely in honey-gold panels separated only by dark seam lines; no second colour and no decoration are needed. The single-hue mosaic read is immediately available to the Gilded Chain or Ferrous Hegemony as a low-complexity high-authority surface language.

- **Engine glow alone can carry all of a design's light identity.** The Endless Space ship (index 87) has three or four orange engine bells as its only emissive element; this makes aft orientation and thrust priority unmistakable while keeping every other surface passive. When a design must communicate direction and role at extreme distance, one saturated engine-glow colour does more work than distributed running lights.

- **Images that show no spacecraft — faction icon, creature in tank, manga megastructure — still yield usable modelling moves.** The Rheinland icon (index 88) provides a hull-marking logic; the Blame! megastructure (index 92) provides fixed-ring-on-spine freighter grammar; the Dune Guild Navigator tank (index 93) provides geometric-container-versus-organic-payload contrast. Every image in this block contributes at least one transferable instruction regardless of whether a recognisable spacecraft is depicted.
