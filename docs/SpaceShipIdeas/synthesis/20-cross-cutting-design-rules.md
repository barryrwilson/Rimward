# How people imagine spaceships — cross-cutting rules

Source base: 238 local reference images (contact sheets `n-`, `c-`, `f-`), 97 fictional ship
renders viewed one by one from fan-wiki image URLs, 16 lead-pass ship studies, and five sourced
link surveys in `../research/`.
This file is the distilled result. Every rule below appears in more than one independent tradition.
Accessed: 2026-08-12

---

## 1. The three-zone law

Almost every credible ship separates into three visible zones along the thrust axis:

| Zone | Contents | Share of length | Evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Bow | Crew, sensors, command | 15–25 % | Donnager, Apocalypse, 1960 Mars nuclear ship (N-076), nuclear shuttle boards (N-116) |
| Mid | Payload, tanks, structure | 45–60 % | Nostromo, Type-9, Sulaco, tug paintings (N-108, N-112) |
| Stern | Drives, radiators, power | 20–30 % | Galactica, Megathron, NERVA and OTV art (N-105, N-106) |

Ships that blend the zones into one smooth mass look like props. Ships that separate them, and
leave visible seams or gaps between them, look operable. The gap itself carries meaning.

**Build rule.** Model each zone as a separate object with its own pivot. Keep a visible seam,
collar, or truss between zones. Never let one continuous panel cross two zones.

## 2. Detail belongs in a band, not on the whole hull

The strongest read-at-distance designs hold 55–80 % of the hull as calm surface and push
the greeble into one band or one joint zone.

- Pride of Hiigara: ~75 % calm slab, ~25 % service band. The band holds every window.
- Galactica: calm armour flanks, rib rhythm as the only mid-form.
- Donnager: dense flanks, but the four radiator fins stay flat and empty, so the outline still reads.
- Nostromo: dense pipes, but the block faces stay flat.

**Build rule.** Draw the band first. Put hangars, windows, catwalks, and antennas inside it.
Target detail density: band 6–10 medium forms per 10 m, calm hull 0–1.

## 3. Emissive area is tiny

Measured by eye across the corpus, lit area sits between 1 % and 6 % of the visible hull.
Warm amber marks habitation. Cool cyan and blue mark machinery and drives. Red marks hazard
and navigation. Green appears only as a faction habit, never as general lighting.

**Build rule.** Cap emissive coverage at 5 % of hull area for every class. Spend it on:
one drive glow, one window band, and a small number of hazard points. Never edge-light every panel.

## 4. Scale comes from repetition, not resolution

Reliable scale cues, in order of strength:

1. A nested object: a rover on a ramp, a shuttle in a bay, a container in a cradle (Carrack, tug boards N-113).
2. A repeated human-size module at constant pitch: window, hatch, rail, light (Khar-Kushan, UNSC Infinity).
3. Hull lettering at constant letter height (Infinity, Galactica squadron number).
4. Structural rhythm: ribs, frames, truss bays (Galactica ribs, ISS truss N-089+).

Greeble density is not a scale cue. Dense small detail on a small hull reads as a busy small hull.

**Build rule.** Fix one human module for the whole fleet: 2.0 m door, 0.8 m window, 1.1 m rail.
Fix one light pitch, for example one marker light per 8 m. Do not scale these with class.

## 5. Four construction logics cover the corpus

| Logic | Visual signature | Corpus examples | RIMWARD fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposed frame | Open trusses, visible tanks and lines, mismatched plates | Rifter, Nostromo, ISS truss, salvage hulls | Freehold Compact, Red Ledger |
| Closed shell | One continuous surface, inset cut-outs, hidden machinery | Imperial Cutter, Apocalypse, Gallente hulls | Gilded Chain, Veridian Combine |
| Repeated module | One part repeated many times along a spine or rail | Sulaco, Infinity, Assembly-style stacks, station modules | Assembly, Ferrous Hegemony |
| Grown body | No panel lines, flow-line detail, slow colour shift | Moya, Leviathan and hive traditions | Beautiful Ones, Unknowables |

A faction becomes legible when it owns one logic and refuses the others. Two factions with the
same logic must then differ by colour habit and by silhouette family.

## 6. Silhouette families that survive at thumbnail size

Counted across the fictional set and the pulp covers: wedge, cigar or blade, disc, spine-and-pods,
dumbbell, cruciform, ring or torus, lens, and blob. Nine families. Pulp covers of the 1920s–1930s
use the cigar, the disc, and the finned rocket almost exclusively; modern design spread the load
into the other families.

**Build rule.** Assign each faction one primary family and one secondary family, then hold the
assignment across all six classes. Class changes the size and the equipment, not the family.

## 7. Function tells the story faster than ornament

Features that made viewers believe a hull, in descending frequency across the corpus:

1. Drive block with real nozzle count and a shielded face.
2. Flat radiator panels that break the outline.
3. Docking collars and ports with the same diameter across the fleet.
4. Cargo bays that are actually open, with something inside.
5. Antenna and sensor masts, thin and fragile against the mass.
6. Landing gear or berthing cradles.
7. A radiation shield disk between reactor and crew (NASA OTV art, N-105).
8. Spin sections for gravity, always with a visible hub and spokes (von Braun wheel, N-097).

Ornament without function — spikes, fins with no radiator role, glowing seams — reads as toy.

## 8. Colour habits, measured

- Field colour is almost always low chroma: grey, sand, bone, olive, rust.
- Accent colour covers 3–8 % of the area and appears in one shape family: stripe, block, or edge trim.
- Precious metal, when used, sits only on edges and only in one motif (Amarr gold arches).
- Weathering is directional. Streaks run aft on ships that fly in atmosphere and radial around vents on ships that do not.

## 9. What the 1970s NASA paintings add that fiction does not

The Marshall Space Flight Center tug and nuclear-shuttle boards (labels N-105 to N-116) are the
most useful freighter and heavy references in the corpus, because they show *systems*, not heroes:

- Seven equal spherical tanks in hexagonal close pack, behind one wide annular radiation shield.
- A long open spine between the payload cluster and the engine, with nothing filling the middle.
- The same vehicle drawn in four mission contexts, proving that role hardware can change while
  the hull silhouette stays constant.
- Explicit crew-module plus propulsion-module seams, drawn as a feature, not hidden.

## 10. Traps observed in the corpus

- Uniform greeble across the whole hull: the ship stops reading and its size collapses.
- Windows scaled with the hull: the ship instantly looks like a toy model.
- Symmetry everywhere: no memorable feature survives at thumbnail size.
- Glow everywhere: night-club effect, no hierarchy, and it kills the silhouette in a bloom pass.
- Fins with no thermal or aerodynamic story: they read as decoration.
- A capital ship with no small craft, no containers, and no berths: nothing gives its size away.
